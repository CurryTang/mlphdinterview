Key Intuition: Why Ordinary Calculus Fails for Brownian Motion

A standard smooth function $f(t)$ has bounded first-order variation and zero quadratic variation ($dt^2 = 0$).

Brownian motion sample paths are nowhere differentiable and have infinite total variation, meaning the curve is infinitely jagged at all zoom levels. However, its quadratic variation converges strictly to $T$. This fundamental fact forces us to retain the second-order Taylor term $\frac{1}{2} f''(W_t) (dW_t)^2 = \frac{1}{2} f''(W_t) dt$ in stochastic calculus.

2D Brownian Motion: Neighborhood Recurrence vs. Point Transience

In discrete $\mathbb{Z}^2$, the simple symmetric random walk hits the exact point $(0,0)$ infinitely many times with probability 1 (null recurrence). In continuous 2D Brownian motion, the set $\{0\}$ has zero capacity, so $P(B_t = (0,0) \text{ for some } t > 0) = 0$ (point transience).

However, 2D Brownian motion will enter any open disk $B_\epsilon(x)$ of arbitrarily small radius $\epsilon > 0$ for unbounded times as $t \to \infty$ (neighborhood recurrence). Furthermore, by Lévy's conformal invariance theorem, any analytic function $f(z)$ transforms a 2D Brownian motion into another 2D Brownian motion with a time change!

Why Quant Finance Uses Itô Calculus Instead of Stratonovich

Itô Integral: Evaluates at the left endpoint $t_i$. It is non-anticipating (adapted), meaning trading decisions at $t_i$ cannot peek into the future increment $\Delta W_i = W_{t_{i+1}} - W_{t_i}$. Therefore, Itô integrals are martingales with zero expected gain from pure noise ($\mathbb{E}[\int H dW] = 0$).

Stratonovich Integral: Evaluates at the midpoint $(t_i + t_{i+1})/2$. It obeys standard Newton-Leibniz chain rules, but it 'anticipates' future information, so its expectation is non-zero ($\mathbb{E} \ne 0$). In financial markets, no trader can trade at the midpoint of an uncompleted future tick!

The Geometry of the Reflection Principle

For any path that reaches barrier $a$ before time $T$ (so $\tau_a \le T$), by the strong Markov property the future increments $W_t - W_{\tau_a}$ for $t \ge \tau_a$ are a new independent Brownian motion starting at $a$.

By spatial symmetry, the path is equally likely to end up above $a$ as below $a$ at terminal time $T$. Reflecting the trajectory across $y = a$ after $\tau_a$ maps every path ending below $a$ (that hit $a$) to a path ending above $a$. Hence, the probability of reaching $a$ is exactly twice the probability of finishing above $a$!

Options Market Making & The Volatility Signature

When an options trader buys a call/put and dynamically Delta-hedges ($-\Delta_t$ shares of stock), they eliminate directional risk ($dW_t$ term disappears via Itô's lemma). The residual daily PnL is pure Gamma vs. Theta:

Every day, the option decays in value due to $\Theta dt < 0$. But as the stock fluctuates, discrete rebalancing buys low and sells high, capturing $\frac{1}{2} S^2 \Gamma (\Delta S)^2 \approx \frac{1}{2} S^2 \Gamma \sigma_R^2 dt$. If realized volatility $\sigma_R > \sigma_I$, the Gamma profit outweighs Theta decay, generating consistent alpha.
